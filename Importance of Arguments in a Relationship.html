Importance of Arguments in a Relationship

Why Quarrels and Arguments, Not Silence, Might Be Healthy in a Romantic Relationship

Written by John Momanyi Omeka · Writer · Researcher · Technology Enthusiast · Thinker · January 27, 2026

Adopting silence or walking out of an argument? Well, that might not be a solution to the problem. Instead, experts reveal that that argument that you so often try to avoid is important for your relationship!

For three years since they got married, Eddy, my best friend, lived with his wife in what seemed to be the most perfect marriage. The two lovebirds never quarreled and they shared the most intimate moments together. It was rare to see Eddy somewhere without his wife. Their relationship was so perfect that individuals who wished to get married through a church wedding chose them as the best couple in their weddings. As a friend, I wondered how on earth a couple could be so perfect! No arguments. No disagreement. Yet, once in a while, my wife and I had visible disagreements and arguments which we had to sit down and sort. Some arguments were over small issues such as my choice of tie, my failure to place my dirty clothes at the rightful place that my wife had set aside without my knowledge, and sometimes, my failure to commend her for a food well-cooked!

One day I gathered confidence and inquired whether Eddy experienced such issues in his home and how he expertly handled them without creating drama. In his response, Eddy stated, “I usually ignore her tantrums. Every time I realize she is being dramatic with some of her ideas, some which may make us argue, I just take a backseat and walk away because I would not like to hurt her with my responses.” Eddy added, “We may remain silent for a day or two but the issue ends there and we continue our normal routine. As a matter of fact, I would not like a simple argument to ruin our marriage or hurt us in any way.” The response by Eddy revealed one thing; that his marriage was not devoid of arguments. In fact, arguments were there but Eddy chose to avoid them and maintain silence instead of facing them.

Fast forward, two years later, Eddy’s marriage faced one of the most trying moments of his life. The arguments he always pushed away had accumulated and they were on the verge of breaking his marriage! His wife accused him of ignoring sensitive discussions and taking her suggestions for granted. On his part, Eddy defended himself saying that he did not feel the issues were serious to warrant discussions. It took the interventions of a church counselor to assist Eddy and his wife face the reality and address the issues soberly. Eddy learned to appreciate arguments and listen whenever his wife raised issues.

Arguments are Normal

The above story is not unique to Eddy. There are several couples who prefer avoiding arguments or quarrels in their relationship because they consider avoidance as the best solution. However, what most forget is the fact that arguments are a normal part of life. Disagreements are what make us humans. However compatible a couple might be, each individual in the relationship comes with their own unique qualities, opinions and thoughts. Such uniqueness also influences individuals’ preferences, desires in a relationship and the way they approach different aspects of life. Consequently, the differences can sometimes clash and result in trivial or serious arguments. How the argument is handled plays a significant role in the direction that a relationship will take.

Generally, arguments or quarrels are ways of demonstrating unmet needs in a relationship. Arguments are usually tailored towards ensuring that such needs are met through adoption of appropriate solutions. Consequently, embracing the arguments and ensuring that you stick around until the end of the argument play a vital role in ensuring the longevity of a relationship. In a study conducted by David Maxfield and Joseph Grenny, the co-author of The New York Times bestseller Crucial Conversations: Tools for Talking When Stakes are High, it was found that couples who embrace healthy conflicts are ten times more likely to have happy and thriving relationships compared to those who chose to ignore difficult conversations. In fact, 80% of the respondents from the study implicated poor communication as a major cause for their last failed relationship. Surprisingly, very few respondents, less than one in in five, were willing to blame themselves for their failed relationships.

Why Embrace Arguments?

Arguing Promotes the Communication of Individual Needs and Desires

Romantic relationships involve people from different backgrounds, with unique ways of looking at life. When these individuals realize that something that makes them fulfilled or meets their desires is missing in a relationship, they seek redress from their partners. While the issues may look small and unnecessary, they are very important to them and wishing them away is interpreted as disregard or ignorance of one’s needs. Choosing to remain silent during an argument or walking away amounts to stonewalling and one partner feels neglected, abandoned, and distant from the person they love. If not addressed, the distance between the couple keeps growing and before they know it, they become emotionally disconnected. When partners embrace the argument and they engage in active listening during the argument, it becomes easier to understand each other’s needs and adopt appropriate measures tailored towards meeting each other’s desires.

Arguing Improves One’s Understanding About their Partner

Arguments are usually a way that people use to vent out their emotions. In the most heated moments, a couple gets to understand the true emotions of their partners however much they might have been hidden. When couples engage in thoughtful and healthy arguments, it is easy to realize how one reacts when they are angry, concerned, or worried about something. This opens the door for emotional connection because partners have an opportunity of understanding the real issue at hand. By so doing, couples can collaboratively find lasting solutions to their issues.

Arguing Promotes Growth Among Partners in a Relationship

Relationships provide an opportunity for people to learn more about each other. Before marriage, people may hide some character that they believe can have a negative impact on their partner, and possibly block their marriage. Unfortunately, some behaviors cannot be hidden for life. In marriage, partners learn to carry each other’s baggage. Each day is a learning process for couples and arguments provide the best opportunity to have better knowledge of one’s partner. Couple learn the things that trigger each other’s reactions, behaviors that are likely to cause harm and the best way of avoiding them. Additionally, as couples have an improved understanding of one another, they learn to confidently their negative and positive feelings.

Furthermore, couples can learn each other’s communication style through their arguments because as they engage each other during their argument, one can open up and state how they approach issues and what they dislike about the approach that their better half adopts. The honest and transparent engagement further strengthens the knowledge partners have about each other. As couple uncover the underlying issues that cause their argument, they learn to address them and by so doing, they keep growing. It is the information that each individual gets from their arguments that helps them to build their relationship. This means that avoiding arguments by maintaining silence or walking away robs individuals an opportunity of having a deeper knowledge of each other. The ignored partner keeps seething with anger and feelings of abandonment which can have significant negative consequences on their psychological wellbeing and in turn affect the whole relationship.

Arguing Enhances a Couple’s Communication Skills

While it is common to argue that silence speaks louder than words, it is essential to note that the deafening impact of silence creates tension and misunderstanding. When disagreement arises in a relationship, verbal communication is the best approach of expressing one’s feelings or contrary perspectives. Keeping quiet does not solve the problem. Instead, leaves one’s partner frustrated and the argument is postponed. Continued postponement of arguments allows them to pile up and when the time comes for the affected partner to bring them all out, their feelings are immeasurable and the long period of neglect and disrespect they kept hidden is revealed through outbursts, uncontrollable cries and in some cases physical fights that can easily destroy a relationship.

Even when one has a valid reason for adopting silence or walking out during an argument, it is essential to communicate verbally to allow the partner understand. For instance, one may walk out during an argument when they realize that they might utter words that are likely to hurt their partner’s feelings or they may end up physically attacking their partner. When such a case suffices, it is essential to politely excuse oneself and schedule a different time when the issue at hand can be addressed. A period when each individual is sober and comfortable to handle the issue. For example, one can request for time to cool down before continuing with the discussion. When such request is communicated, then it shows respect for the partner and lets them know that they are not being abandoned. Be sure to also keep the promise by returning and ensuring that the conflict is handled to its successful conclusion.
